\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate the proposed hyperbolic morphing procedure on four cut-open
surface embeddings: a genus--2 triangulation, a genus--2 cellular graph with
quadrilateral and pentagonal faces, a genus--3 triangulation, and a genus--3
cellular graph with quadrilateral and pentagonal faces.  In all cases the
embedding is represented in the Poincare disk, with the side-pairing Mobius
transformations encoding the quotient surface.  The genus--2 examples use a
regular octagonal fundamental domain for the Bolza surface, while the genus--3
examples use a regular 14-gonal fundamental domain for the Klein quartic.

The combinatorial sizes of the four test cases are listed in
Table~\ref{tab:morph-topology-counts}.  We omit the construction details of the
initial planar graphs here; for the purposes of the morphing experiment, the
important distinction is whether the input is a triangulation or a cellular
decomposition with higher-order faces.

\paragraph{Endpoint embeddings.}
For each graph we first compute two endpoint embeddings by minimizing a
hyperbolic Dirichlet energy.  The source endpoint uses scalar edge weights
$w_e \equiv 1$.  The target endpoint uses a non-uniform scalar weight field in
which the first half of the edge list has weight $10$, and the remaining half
has weight $1$.  Both endpoints are computed with the hyperbolic harmonic-map
solver in the Poincare disk, subject to the same fixed fundamental-domain
corner vertices and the same side-pairing Mobius constraints.

We write a vertex position as a complex number $z_i \in \D$.  The Poincare disk
metric is conformal to the Euclidean metric with factor
\[
    \lambda_z = \frac{2}{1-|z|^2}.
\]
For $x,y\in \D$, Mobius addition is
\[
    x \oplus y = \frac{x+y}{1+\overline{x}y},
\]
and the hyperbolic distance is
\[
    d_{\D}(x,y) =
    2 \operatorname{arctanh}\left|(-x)\oplus y\right|.
\]
The logarithm and exponential maps used in the gradient descent are
\[
    \log_x(y)
    =
    \frac{2}{\lambda_x}
    \frac{\operatorname{arctanh}|\delta|}{|\delta|}
    \delta,
    \qquad
    \delta=(-x)\oplus y,
\]
with $\log_x(x)=0$, and
\[
    \exp_x(v)
    =
    x \oplus
    \left(
        \tanh\left(\frac{\lambda_x |v|}{2}\right)
        \frac{v}{|v|}
    \right),
\]
with $\exp_x(0)=x$.

For scalar endpoint weights, the energy is
\[
    E(z)=\frac12\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E} w_{ij}\,d_{\D}(z_i,z_j)^2.
\]
The Riemannian gradient contribution at the two endpoints of an edge is
\[
    \nabla_{z_i}E_{\{i,j\}}=-w_{ij}\log_{z_i}(z_j),
    \qquad
    \nabla_{z_j}E_{\{i,j\}}=-w_{ij}\log_{z_j}(z_i).
\]
For directed mean-value weights, the morphing solve uses directed coefficients
$\omega_{ij}$ and $\omega_{ji}$ in the gradient.  The scalar coefficient used
in the displayed Dirichlet energy is the symmetrized value
\[
    a_{ij}=\frac{\omega_{ij}+\omega_{ji}}{2}.
\]
With $d_{ij}=d_{\D}(z_i,z_j)$, the edge contribution is
\[
    \nabla_{z_i}E_{\{i,j\}}
    =
    -\omega_{ij}\frac{\sinh d_{ij}}{d_{ij}}\log_{z_i}(z_j),
    \qquad
    \nabla_{z_j}E_{\{i,j\}}
    =
    -\omega_{ji}\frac{\sinh d_{ij}}{d_{ij}}\log_{z_j}(z_i),
\]
where $\sinh d_{ij}/d_{ij}$ is set to $1$ when $d_{ij}=0$.  The update of each
free root vertex is performed by the exponential map,
\[
    z_i^{(m+1)}
    =
    \exp_{z_i^{(m)}}\left(-\eta\,\nabla_{z_i}E\right),
\]
while fixed corner vertices remain fixed.  Slave vertices on paired sides are
not independently updated; after each root update, their positions are resolved
from the side-pairing Mobius transformations.  If a constrained vertex is
$z_s=M_{s\leftarrow r}(z_r)$, its gradient contribution is pulled back to the
root vertex through the differential of $M_{s\leftarrow r}$ before the root
update is applied.

\paragraph{Hyperbolic convexity check.}
For the cellular examples, the higher-order faces must remain geodesically
convex for mean-value coordinates to be well-defined.  This is checked with
tangent directions of Poincare geodesics, not Euclidean chord angles.  Let
$p=(x_p,y_p)$ and $q=(x_q,y_q)$ be two adjacent vertices.  If
$x_p y_q-y_p x_q=0$, the geodesic is a Euclidean diameter and the tangent at
$p$ toward $q$ is
\[
    \tau_{p\to q}
    =
    \frac{q-p}{\|q-p\|}.
\]
Otherwise the geodesic is the Euclidean circle orthogonal to $\partial\D$ and
passing through $p$ and $q$.  Its center $c=(c_x,c_y)$ is found by solving
\[
    2c\cdot p=\|p\|^2+1,\qquad
    2c\cdot q=\|q\|^2+1,
\]
equivalently, for $\Delta=x_p y_q-y_p x_q$,
\[
    c_x =
    \frac{(\|p\|^2+1)y_q-(\|q\|^2+1)y_p}{2\Delta},
    \qquad
    c_y =
    \frac{x_p(\|q\|^2+1)-x_q(\|p\|^2+1)}{2\Delta}.
\]
The tangent line is perpendicular to the radius $p-c$, so the two possible
unit tangents are
\[
    \pm\frac{(-(y_p-c_y),\,x_p-c_x)}{\|p-c\|}.
\]
We choose the sign for which $p+\epsilon\tau_{p\to q}$ is closer to $q$ than
$p-\epsilon\tau_{p\to q}$ for a small $\epsilon>0$.  For a face
$(v_0,\ldots,v_{\ell-1})$, the signed turn at $v_k$ is
\[
    \theta_k =
    \operatorname{atan2}
    \left(
        \det(-\tau_{v_k\to v_{k-1}},\,\tau_{v_k\to v_{k+1}}),
        \left\langle -\tau_{v_k\to v_{k-1}},\,\tau_{v_k\to v_{k+1}}\right\rangle
    \right).
\]
The face is accepted as geodesically convex when all $\theta_k$ have the face
orientation sign.  The minimized cellular endpoints used below passed this
tangent-angle convexity test and had no Poincare-geodesic edge crossings.

\paragraph{Mean-value directed weights.}
After computing the two endpoint convex embeddings, we discard the scalar
endpoint weights and recompute directed mean-value edge weights.  Around a
center vertex $i$, all neighbors are first attached into the same local star
using the side-pairing Mobius transformations.  This is essential at polygon
corners: a corner can appear to have low valence in the cut domain, but its
quotient-surface star includes copies attached across paired sides.

The star is moved by a disk isometry sending $z_i$ to the origin.  For a
neighbor occurrence $j$, let
\[
    u_j=\frac{z_j-z_i}{1-\overline{z_i}z_j}
    =
    r_j e^{\sqrt{-1}\phi_j},
    \qquad
    \rho_j=2\operatorname{arctanh} r_j.
\]
After sorting the neighbor occurrences by angle, let $\alpha_j^{-}$ and
$\alpha_j^{+}$ be the adjacent angular gaps before and after occurrence $j$.
The unnormalized hyperbolic mean-value directed weight is
\[
    \omega_{ij}
    =
    \frac{
        \tan(\alpha_j^{-}/2)+\tan(\alpha_j^{+}/2)
    }{
        \sinh \rho_j
    }.
\]
When multiple attached occurrences have the same angular direction, the
numerator is shared equally among them.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\fbox{
\begin{minipage}{0.94\linewidth}
\textbf{Algorithm 1: directed mean-value weights on the quotient star.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item For each root vertex $i$, collect all incident neighbor occurrences in
the quotient-surface star.  If $i$ lies on the cut boundary, include the
copies attached by the side-pairing Mobius transformations.
\item Move the center $z_i$ to the origin by $u=(z-z_i)/(1-\overline{z_i}z)$.
Record the polar coordinates $u_j=r_j e^{\sqrt{-1}\phi_j}$ for each attached
neighbor occurrence.
\item Sort the occurrences by angle $\phi_j$ and compute the adjacent angular
gaps $\alpha_j^{-}$ and $\alpha_j^{+}$.
\item Assign the directed weight
$\omega_{ij}=(\tan(\alpha_j^{-}/2)+\tan(\alpha_j^{+}/2))/\sinh\rho_j$, where
$\rho_j=2\operatorname{arctanh}r_j$.  If several occurrences share the same
direction, split the numerator equally among them.
\item Repeat the same computation at every root vertex, producing directed
weights $\omega_{ij}$ and $\omega_{ji}$ on each undirected edge $\{i,j\}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{minipage}}
\caption{Computation of the directed mean-value weights used at the source and
target endpoint embeddings.}
\label{alg:directed-mean-value-weights}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Morphing solve.}
Let $\omega_{ij}^{0}$ denote the directed mean-value weight from vertex $i$ to
vertex $j$ in the all-one endpoint embedding, and let $\omega_{ij}^{1}$ denote
the corresponding directed weight in the half-10 endpoint embedding.  For
frame $k=0,\ldots,24$, with $t_k=k/24$, we interpolate the directed weights
linearly,
\[
    \omega_{ij}(t_k)
    =
    (1-t_k)\,\omega_{ij}^{0}
    +
    t_k\,\omega_{ij}^{1}.
\]
The scalar edge coefficient recorded for the frame energy is
$a_{ij}(t_k)=(\omega_{ij}(t_k)+\omega_{ji}(t_k))/2$.
Each frame is obtained by solving the hyperbolic harmonic-map equations with
these interpolated directed weights.  The previous frame is used as the initial
iterate for the next frame.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\fbox{
\begin{minipage}{0.94\linewidth}
\textbf{Algorithm 2: mean-value-coordinate morphing.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item Compute the source endpoint embedding $Z^0$ by minimizing the scalar
Dirichlet energy with $w_e\equiv 1$.
\item Compute the target endpoint embedding $Z^1$ by minimizing the scalar
Dirichlet energy with $w_e=10$ on the first half of the edge list and $w_e=1$
on the remaining half.
\item Use Algorithm~\ref{alg:directed-mean-value-weights} to compute directed
weights $\omega^0$ from $Z^0$ and $\omega^1$ from $Z^1$.
\item Set the initial iterate for frame $0$ to $Z^0$.
\item For $k=0,\ldots,24$, set $t_k=k/24$ and
$\omega_{ij}(t_k)=(1-t_k)\omega_{ij}^{0}+t_k\omega_{ij}^{1}$.
\item Set $a_{ij}(t_k)=(\omega_{ij}(t_k)+\omega_{ji}(t_k))/2$ for the scalar
energy coefficient on edge $\{i,j\}$.
\item Starting from the previous frame, repeatedly compute the directed
gradient, pull constrained-vertex gradient contributions back to their root
vertices, and update each free root by
$z_i\leftarrow\exp_{z_i}(-\eta\nabla_{z_i}E)$.
\item Stop the frame solve when the maximum hyperbolic displacement between
successive iterates is below the prescribed tolerance, i.e.
$\max_i d_{\D}(z_i^{(m+1)},z_i^{(m)})<\varepsilon$.
\item Resolve the constrained side vertices by their Mobius transformations and
write the resulting embedding as frame $k$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{minipage}}
\caption{Frame-by-frame morphing with interpolated directed mean-value
weights.}
\label{alg:mean-value-morph}
\end{figure}

Across all four examples, the generated frame sequence remained embedded in the
Poincare disk: no frame had Poincare-geodesic edge crossings.  For the two
cellular morphs, every displayed and generated frame also passed the
tangent-angle convexity check on all quadrilateral and pentagonal faces.  These
experiments indicate that the same endpoint-minimization and directed
mean-value interpolation procedure applies both to triangulations and to
cellular decompositions with convex higher-order faces, and works for both the
genus--2 Bolza surface and the genus--3 Klein quartic examples.
